      Where these numbers come from
      • With the planner: after each morning, the van's delivery log records when it reached each stop and how old each tray was. Profit is what the cafés paid for what was actually delivered (after freshness discounts and refused trays), minus the van, the oven and the ingredients.
      • Your old way: your rule of thumb (medium oven, the first 8 orders in, stops in wanted-by order, trays in before you leave and again halfway), planned on the same orders and driven through the same morning's traffic. The two lines differ only in how the morning was planned.
      • Freshness: the share of kolaches handed over less than 15 minutes after their tray came out of the oven.
      • Worst stop: each morning, how many minutes the plan's arrival time was off at the stop it got most wrong; each week shows the middle morning. It drops as the planner learns real Dallas drive times from the logs.
      • Honest note: these mornings are simulated. Orders and traffic come from a seeded generator, not real cafés (see the bottom of the page).

      How you planned before the planner

      Every night you planned the morning by hand from the order sheet, with a phone map app, in about 45 minutes (our estimate). Your rule of thumb:

      1. Always rent the medium oven. 48 kolaches at once, $45 an hour, whatever the orders look like.
      2. First come, first served, up to 8 stops. Orders are taken in the order they arrived; the 9th order and later get a no, even if they are close by and pay well.
      3. Drive in wanted-by order. Earliest wanted-by time first, wherever the café is, using the map app's drive times (traffic included).
      4. Trays in before you leave, and again halfway. The first half of the stops' trays go in 18 minutes before you leave; the rest go in at the halfway stop.

      This is the rule written down in decisions B1 and B2. We made it as fair as we could: it uses the same traffic-aware drive times as the planner, so the comparison shows only what is new.
